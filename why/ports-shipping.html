Why Sri Lanka for shipping

Ten nautical miles from the world's busiest lane.

Colombo handled a record 8.29 million TEU in 2025. It sits a few miles off the main east west route, which is why so much Indian cargo changes ships here. Indian capital already runs a terminal and owns the largest shipyard.

The India angle

What this means for Indian businesses.

If your containers already transit Colombo, an entity here gives you control of the relay. Vizhinjam will compete for some volume, which makes a two port strategy smarter, not redundant.

₹
Trade in rupees

Invoice in rupees. Skip the dollar.

Indian and Sri Lankan businesses can invoice, pay and settle trade in Indian rupees. It runs through RBI's Special Rupee Vostro Accounts, and Sri Lanka lists the rupee among its designated foreign currencies. No dollar conversion at either end, less currency risk, and books kept in the currency you already report in.

  • ₹ invoicesExports and imports priced, paid and settled in INR.
  • ₹ creditSince October 2025, Indian banks can lend rupees to Sri Lankan businesses and banks for cross border trade.
  • Faster setupSince July 2026, Indian banks open rupee vostro accounts for overseas banks without a separate RBI approval.

Questions

Asked by Indian founders and buyers.

Why is Colombo a transshipment hub for India?

Colombo is a few nautical miles from the main east west shipping lane and has deep water berths, so large vessels call there and smaller feeders carry cargo to and from Indian ports.

Which Indian companies operate in Sri Lankan ports?

Adani operates the Colombo West Container Terminal with John Keells, and Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders acquired 51% of Colombo Dockyard in 2026.
