India Sri Lanka Free Trade Agreement (ISFTA) explained

India and Sri Lanka have traded under a free trade agreement since 2000. About four out of five tariff lines are duty free both ways, which is why Sri Lankan apparel, pepper and vanaspati, and Indian machinery, pharma and vehicles cross the strait so easily.

The basics

  • Signed: 28 December 1998. In force: 1 March 2000.
  • Duty free: 4,228 of 5,224 six digit tariff lines, about 81%. India finished cutting tariffs in 2003 and Sri Lanka in 2008.
  • Negative lists: each side keeps a list of sensitive goods with no concession. The original lists covered 429 lines for India and 1,180 for Sri Lanka.

Rules of origin

To qualify, a product needs at least 35% domestic value addition and must change its tariff heading at the four digit level. If Indian inputs are used, Sri Lankan value addition can drop to 25%, as long as India and Sri Lanka together add at least 35%. Exporters claim the benefit with a certificate of origin.

How big is the trade?

In calendar 2025, India exported about USD 4.38 billion to Sri Lanka and imported about USD 1.04 billion, according to India's High Commission in Colombo. China edged past India as Sri Lanka's biggest trading partner in 2025, and India was the second largest source of foreign direct investment that year, after Singapore.

What is next: ETCA and the upgrade

Talks on a wider Economic and Technology Cooperation Agreement went through 14 rounds, the last in July 2024, then stalled. In August 2026 the two governments agreed a joint working group to update the FTA itself, and Sri Lanka has said it wants to renegotiate terms. Watch for changes to services, investment and the negative lists.

  • Get a certificate of origin for every shipment.

Quick answers
What is the India Sri Lanka Free Trade Agreement?

A bilateral FTA signed in December 1998 and in force since 1 March 2000. About 81% of tariff lines, 4,228 of 5,224, are duty free between the two countries, with negative lists for sensitive goods.

What are the ISFTA rules of origin?

At least 35% domestic value addition plus a change of tariff heading at the four digit level. Where Indian inputs are used, Sri Lankan value addition can be 25% if combined value addition reaches 35%.

Can Sri Lankan tea enter India duty free?

Under a quota. India allows 15,000 tonnes of Sri Lankan tea a year at a concessional 7.5% duty under the ISFTA.

What happened to ETCA between India and Sri Lanka?

ETCA talks went through 14 rounds, the last in July 2024, then stalled. In August 2026 the two countries agreed a joint working group to update the existing FTA instead.

Who is Sri Lanka's biggest trading partner?

In 2025, China narrowly overtook India. India was second, with about USD 5.4 billion of two way trade, and the second largest source of foreign direct investment.
